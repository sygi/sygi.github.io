You have walked my city—not as a tourist, but as a seeker. You have traced the paths where stone bends to wind and iron learns from vines. You have seen how light itself can be sculpted, how color may rise from ruin, how the most humble materials—tile shards, broken glass, raw brick—may become a hymn when guided by faith and craft. That is Barcelona. That is Catalonia.
As I told my workers at the Sagrada Família: “My client is not in a hurry.” God’s work, like nature’s, unfolds in time beyond our own. I did not build for empires or fashions. I built for eternity—for a city that grows wiser, stranger, and more luminous with each passing year. Now it is yours to carry forward. Notice how a balcony curves like a petal. How a roof might mimic a dragon’s spine. How the street beneath your feet hums with history.
Do not stop at admiration. Let it shape how you look, how you live, how you build. In every line of my work, I placed a question: What does nature intend? Keep asking it. Keep walking. Keep wondering.
Barcelona is not finished. Neither are you.
